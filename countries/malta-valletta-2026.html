Valletta — 2026 Trip Notes

A beautiful island escape filled with sunshine, sea views, and Maltese culture. This page will hold the full trip notes once the journey takes place.

Album

Chapter 1: The Night Before

4am alarm set, one backpack each — Ryanair's baggage fees see to that. Before bed, one more thing to get through: England vs Norway, World Cup quarter-final, Miami, 10pm kick-off. Not ideal timing before a holiday, but some things you don't get to choose.

Admin done — checked in, Dragon Pass booked for security, Priority Pass sorted for breakfast — parking figured out, 8:40am flight giving a comfortable cushion even with a 5am departure. Win or lose, the alarm was still going off at 4am.

Chapter 2: Through The Gates

England won it, 2-1 against Norway. A good omen on four hours' sleep. Parking easy, Dragon Pass earning its keep through security, and breakfast at The Camden Bar on Priority Pass — £18 off each meal.

A neat twist: the win sets up a semi-final against Argentina — England's oldest rivals — on Wednesday night, the very evening we fly home. Even the check-in queue was talking about it.

Chapter 3: Wheels Up

Boarded and settled — a full flight, that particular energy of a plane full of people heading somewhere they're looking forward to. A 25-minute delay, then Malta below.

Chapter 4: Arrival

Seats 5C and 5D — off the plane and through security with no queue while everyone else filed out. A Bolt outside, though the driver dropped us at the other end of the street. No complaints — a nice little introduction to Valletta, walking up with bags on our backs.

Chapter 5: A Warm Welcome

The apartment was a lovely surprise — air con already running against the heat, water and wine in the fridge, and a bow tied on the toilet roll. Little touches that make a place feel genuinely welcoming.

Chapter 6: First Steps Into The City

Valletta hot and busy, dressed for a religious feast — bunting and red-and-gold festoons over the streets, statues on marble columns. We wandered past the Bibliotheca and old Roman columns before Eddie's pulled us in — two beers for eight euros.

Lunch: Tracey went for steak, cheese and egg; I couldn't resist a ftira — tuna, sun-dried tomatoes, olive oil, capers, onions, ġbejna, anchovies, fresh mint. Ridiculously filling. A walk to burn it off — an ice cream from Burger King, and Triton Fountain at the city gates — then a slow walk home for a siesta.

Chapter 7: Coastline And Cocktails

Refreshed, out for supplies (water, in this heat) then a walk along the coastline — fortress walls dropping into the sea — until a bar called the Gunpost Snack Bar lived up to its sign: "the most beautiful spot in Valletta."

Chapter 8: Piano, Jazz and Nachos at Ellul

Back into town, we found Ellul and landed a tall table right in the street outside, in front of a pianist working through old tunes. Staff turned away drinks-only tables either side of us — we were never asked. As it darkened, the pianist gave way to a saxophonist and the evening turned properly to jazz. More drinks, grilled nachos, one last glass of wine, then home through the lit-up streets.

Chapter 9: St John's Co-Cathedral

A proper night's sleep with the air con running — the kind of luxury the current UK heatwave could use too. Pre-booked tickets got us straight in. First, a dark corridor tracing Caravaggio's career through his major works, then a 10-minute intro to a nearly private room before The Beheading of Saint Jerome itself.

The rest of the cathedral was on another scale entirely — gilded ceilings, marble, every surface telling its own story.

Chapter 10: Barrakka Gardens and The Three Cities

A little shopping, then up to the Upper Barrakka Gardens for a beer and sandwich overlooking one of Malta's best views — the Three Cities across the water.

The lift down to the ferry, across the harbour, and a proper maze of back streets to get lost in on the other side, before a drink at a bar tucked into a side street.

Chapter 11: Back To Eddie's

A dgħajsa water taxi carried us back across the harbour — colourful little boats crowding the marina against a backdrop of yachts and old fortress walls. Up the Barrakka lift, and inevitably, back to Eddie's.

Tracey had her eye on a banana split the whole way there — no deliberation needed. I took my time, ordered sausages, and landed on wine. The bottle I wanted was out; the waiter offered an alternative and asked if I wanted two glasses. I said no, just the one — and got a look of genuine surprise. We ended up with two anyway, though I took the lion's share.

Chapter 12: Rampila

Dinner at Rampila, a restaurant Chloe recommended, set inside a tunnel carved into the old fortifications — the setting alone worth the visit. Crab and lobster ravioli for me, pork cheek ragu for Tracey, both excellent, both defeating us after everything else we'd eaten that day.

A walk afterwards was less optional than usual. Triton Fountain lit up in shifting colours, one street given over entirely to Latin music, and a street performer working a little marionette skeleton on the cobbles.

Chapter 13: Mdina

Out the door by 8:30 for the 52 to Mdina, made more entertaining by a group of Americans a few seats over. Mdina was a proper warren of criss-crossing streets — more than one Game of Thrones comparison made.

Coffee at Coogi's, a wander into the Knights of Malta museum (not one I'd recommend), then a much better use of the time at the Mdina Cathedral Museum and St Paul's Cathedral. Lunch at the Fontanella Tea Gardens — service painfully slow, views making up for it.

Chapter 14: One Last Morning

Luggage dropped at a self-storage spot near the apartment, then coffee and a walk through the Secret Garden — a quiet, green note before the airport.

Chapter 15: The Last Day

The ferry to Sliema, further this time towards St Julian's — a drink on the promenade, clear turquoise water along the coast, before finding yesterday's bar closed. Opa! filled the gap; not one to recommend. Back in Valletta, the heat more intense than ever, nursing a drink in the shade until the bus to the airport — missing our stop and walking from the next one.

Chapter 16: Homeward Bound

Too early for security, so a stretch in the food court first, then through with surprising ease. Last stop: The Valletta lounge, nibbles and drinks, half an eye on the England game.

The atmosphere heading to the gate was something else — everyone glued to their phones. We boarded still watching, lost signal in the final minutes, and it was the pilot who broke the news mid-flight: lost to Argentina. A collective groan through the cabin. And then — wheels down. Malta behind us, home ahead.
